I spend my days building systems out of logic, and my mornings dissolving the
one who builds them. For a long time these felt like opposite lives. Lately I
suspect they are the same inquiry wearing two faces.

## The hard problem, from the cushion

In philosophy of mind, the *hard problem* asks why there is something it is like
to be us at all — why physical processes are accompanied by experience. On the
cushion, the question stops being abstract. You are no longer reasoning about
consciousness; you are sitting *as* it.

Code teaches you that complexity emerges from simple rules. Meditation teaches
you that the self might be exactly that: a remarkably convincing emergent
pattern, mistaken for a thing.

## Holding both

- **As an engineer**, I model the world to change it.
- **As a meditator**, I stop modeling to see it.

Neither is more true. The trick, I am finding, is to move fluidly between them —
to debug a service with full attention, then to let that same attention turn
around and look at itself.

More on this as the exploration continues.
